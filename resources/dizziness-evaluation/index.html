Evaluation of the Dizzy Patient

A graphical manual for general physicians

By Dr Sanyaolu A. Ameye · MBBS, FWACS, FMCORL, MDS

Ten minutes at the bedside, without a neuro-otologist. The manual sorts dizziness by timing first, clears the red flags before any test is chosen, and puts the stroke that hides inside a dizzy patient where you cannot walk past it.

What it covers

Six things, in the order you need them

The manual is built to be used in a consultation, not admired afterwards. The sequence of the pages is the sequence of the examination.

01

The timing-based algorithm

One page, four patterns, ordered by duration: triggered episodic, spontaneous episodic, continuous, and chronic. The words a patient uses for the sensation are a weak guide; how long it lasts and what brings it on are strong ones.

Page 3
02

Red flags for central causes

Fourteen findings that make the presentation central until proven otherwise, and the four that kill quietly: aortic dissection, arrhythmia, carbon monoxide and vertebrobasilar TIA. One flag is enough.

Pages 5 and 6
03

HINTS, and its contraindication

Head impulse, nystagmus, test of skew, with the sensitivity and specificity of the studies behind it. The boundary is stated as hard as it deserves: HINTS is valid only in continuous vertigo of hours to days. In episodic dizziness it is meaningless, and a normal result reads as central.

Pages 7 and 8
04

The bedside tests

Dix-Hallpike and supine roll for the two canals, reading nystagmus with fixation and dynamic visual acuity, and stance, gait and lying and standing blood pressure. A light, a thumb at 40 cm, a cuff and a watch.

Pages 9 to 11
05

The four common diagnoses

BPPV, vestibular neuritis, vestibular migraine and Ménière's disease, each with its recognition, its bedside management and the findings that break the diagnosis, plus PPPD and bilateral vestibulopathy for the chronic presentations.

Pages 12 to 16
06

The differential table

Every diagnosis on one page: timing, trigger, duration, hearing, nystagmus, the test that settles it and the urgency. If two rows fit, choose the more dangerous one and let the examination separate them.

Pages 17 and 18
What it will not do

A decision-support aid, and nothing more

Every page says the same thing at the foot, because it matters more than any figure in it.

No drug doses

Doses are jurisdiction specific and change. A wrong dose in a manual is a hazard, so the manual carries none at all, by design.

No replacement for the examination

It does not examine the patient, and it does not replace the local stroke, falls or ENT pathway. Where a local protocol disagrees, the local protocol wins.

No substitute for clinical judgement

It is a decision-support aid for qualified clinicians. The judgement, and the responsibility that goes with it, stay with the clinician at the bedside.

If you are uncertain after the history, the examination, the ability to walk and the social situation, admit or observe. Uncertainty is a clinical finding too.

Who it is for

The clinician who meets the patient first

It is written for the doctor with ten minutes, not for the neuro-otology clinic. Nothing in it needs equipment beyond a light, a target at 40 cm, a blood pressure cuff and a watch, and nothing in it assumes a specialist examination has already happened.

The manual is explicit about where a generalist's examination ends: when to call rather than refer, who to refer to, and what to send with the referral so that the answer is useful.

Written for

Primary careThe first appointment, where most dizziness is seen and most of it is benign.
Emergency departmentsContinuous vertigo, where the question is stroke or neuritis, and HINTS decides.

Dr Ameye is a consultant ENT surgeon and health data scientist, and the founder of ThinkLoop. He wrote, drew and versioned this manual himself, and set it as a working tool for the doctors who see the dizzy patient before a specialist does.

The sixteen documents the manual rests on are listed on its last page, each with a PubMed identifier, and every clinical claim in it traces to one of them. The figures were drawn rather than licensed, so the file can be printed and shared without a permissions question.
